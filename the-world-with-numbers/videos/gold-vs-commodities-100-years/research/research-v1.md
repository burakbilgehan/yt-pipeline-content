## Executive Summary

This video prices every major asset in **gold terms** (asset/gold ratio) over 100 years (1925–2025), stripping away inflation to reveal real purchasing power changes. The central finding is striking: **gold has crushed almost everything**. Over a century, platinum lost 94% of its value in gold terms, oil lost 81%, copper lost 84%, and silver lost 29%. Even the mighty Dow Jones — up ~26,000% in dollar terms — gained only 30% when priced in gold. The S&P 500 fared better at +141%, but that's still shockingly modest for 100 years.

The data tells a story of two eras: the gold standard era (1925–1971) where gold was fixed and ratios reflected pure supply/demand, and the fiat era (1971–2025) where gold's liberation exposed the true depreciation of everything priced in paper money.

## Key Narrative Arc

### Act 1: The Setup — What "Priced in Gold" Means (30 sec)
- Quick explanation: divide any asset's price by gold's price to get the ratio
- Example: If oil is $80/barrel and gold is $4,000/oz, oil costs 0.02 oz of gold per barrel
- Why this matters: strips away inflation, shows REAL purchasing power
- Visual: animated formula, then chart preview teaser

### Act 2: The Gold Standard Era, 1925–1971 (45 sec)
- Gold was FIXED — $20.67/oz until 1934, then $35/oz until 1971
- During this era, ratios moved based on pure commodity supply/demand
- **1929 crash**: Dow/Gold crashed from 18.4 to 2.0 — stocks lost 89% in gold terms
- **1934 FDR revaluation**: Gold jumped 69% overnight ($20.67 → $35), every ratio dropped ~40% instantly
- **Bretton Woods (1944)**: Gold became the global monetary anchor
- 1960s: Dow/Gold climbed to 27.7 as stocks boomed, but gold was artificially cheap
- Platinum was the king — trading at 2.5x to 5.6x gold's price

### Act 3: The Nixon Shock and Gold's Liberation, 1971–1980 (45 sec)
- **1971**: Nixon ends gold convertibility — THE pivotal moment
- Gold freed from $35, begins explosive rise
- ALL ratios collapse: Dow/Gold from 27.7 (1965) → 1.6 (1980)
- Oil embargo (1973): both oil AND gold surge, but gold wins
- 1980 peak: Gold hits $850/oz, Dow/Gold at historic low 1.6:1
- Hunt Brothers silver squeeze: silver hits $49.45 (Jan 1980)
- This was "the great gold decade" — gold outperformed everything

### Act 4: The Great Reversal, 1980–2000 (45 sec)
- Longest stock bull market in history
- Gold enters 20-year bear market, falls from $850 to $253
- Dow/Gold EXPLODES from 1.6 to 39.5 — a 24x expansion
- S&P 500/Gold from 0.23 to 5.38 — a 23x expansion
- Even commodities gained vs gold during this period
- Oil/Gold ratio recovered as oil prices stayed stable while gold fell
- Peak: January 14, 2000 — Dow/Gold hits 41.3:1 (highest ever recorded)
- This era "proved" gold was a relic... or so people thought

### Act 5: Gold's Revenge, 2000–2025 (45 sec)
- Dot-com crash, 9/11, Iraq War → gold starts new bull run
- 2008 Financial Crisis: gold surges as safe haven
- 2011: Gold nears $1,900, Dow/Gold back to 6.5
- 2020 COVID: Oil goes NEGATIVE (-$37/barrel), gold hits $2,075
- 2024–2025: Gold breaks $4,000/oz, then keeps going
- ALL commodity/gold ratios at or near historic lows
- Platinum falls below gold for the first time (around 2015) and never recovers
- Silver surge 2025-2026: hits record $69.74 (Dec 2025), crosses $100/oz (Jan 2026)

## Surprising Findings (for emphasis in video)

1. **Platinum's collapse is the biggest story.** In 1925, platinum was worth 5.6x gold. Today it's worth 0.31x gold. A 94% loss. If you bought platinum in 1925 expecting it to hold its premium, you'd have lost almost everything in real terms.

2. **The Dow's "26,000% gain" is mostly inflation.** Priced in gold, the Dow gained just 30% in 100 years. That's 0.26% per year in real terms. The narrative that "stocks always go up" needs an asterisk.

3. **The S&P 500 is the only clear winner**, up 141% in gold terms. But even this is just 0.88% per year compounded — hardly spectacular.

4. **Oil priced in gold is at ALL-TIME LOWS.** In 1925, one ounce of gold bought 12.3 barrels of oil. Today it buys 64.5 barrels. Oil has gotten 5x cheaper in real terms.

5. **The Dow/Gold ratio is perfectly cyclical.** It swings between ~2 (gold peaks) and ~40 (stock peaks). Currently at 10, it's mid-cycle — potentially headed either way.

6. **1971 is the dividing line for everything.** Pre-1971, gold was artificially fixed. Post-1971, gold has relentlessly outperformed commodities. The data suggests fiat currency debasement is the main driver.

7. **Silver's recent surge ($100+/oz in 2026) is unprecedented**, yet in gold terms, silver is STILL below its 1925 ratio. Even at $100, silver hasn't kept up with gold.
