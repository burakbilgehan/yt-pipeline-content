## Act 1: The Hook & Setup (0:00 – 1:00)

[VOICEOVER]
The Dow Jones has gained twenty-six thousand percent since 1925. Sounds like the greatest investment in history, right? But what if I told you — when you price the Dow in gold — it has only doubled. That's it. One hundred years. A hundred and thirty-two percent. Less than one percent a year.

Here's the idea: forget dollars. Dollars lose value every single year. Instead, let's price everything — stocks, oil, silver, platinum — in gold. How many ounces of gold does it take to buy one unit of each asset? This strips away all the inflation noise and shows you what actually gained real value — and what didn't.

I crunched a hundred years of daily data — over a hundred and seventy-five thousand data points — and the results tell a very different story than Wall Street wants you to hear. Let's race them.

## Act 2: Boom, Bust & Revaluation — 1925–1944 (1:00 – 2:30)

[VOICEOVER]
For the first half of this story, gold's price was fixed by the government. Twenty dollars and sixty-seven cents an ounce. So every ratio here is a pure reflection of supply and demand — no inflation tricks.

In 1925, the Dow-to-Gold ratio starts at six. By September nineteen twenty-nine, it triples — reaching eighteen point four. The roaring twenties on the chart look like a rocket.

Then the floor drops out. The Great Depression wipes out eighty-eight percent of the Dow's value in gold terms. The ratio crashes from eighteen down to two. Two. Millions lose everything. Banks collapse. The lowest point for stocks relative to gold — until the nineteen eighties.

In 1934, Roosevelt does something radical — he devalues the dollar against gold. Overnight, gold jumps from twenty dollars sixty-seven cents to thirty-five dollars an ounce. The Dow-to-Gold ratio settles at about three.

Then comes World War Two. Markets freeze — the ratio hovers around three for years. But in 1944, forty-four nations gather at Bretton Woods and create a new global financial system. Every major currency is pegged to the dollar — and the dollar is pegged to gold.

## Act 3: The Post-War Boom — 1944–1971 (2:30 – 3:34)

[VOICEOVER]
The war ends — and America doesn't slow down. Factories that built tanks now build cars. Europe and Japan are rebuilding from rubble. Stocks take off. The Dow recovers from three back to four, then ten, then twenty.

New players enter the race. The Nikkei joins in 1949 — starting so small it's barely a pixel on the chart. Remember that tiny line. It's going to matter. Copper enters in 1948 — invisible next to the Dow. The DAX arrives in 1959, riding West Germany's economic miracle. Oil and Platinum only appear in 1968 — and platinum starts above gold. The only asset to ever do that.

By the mid-sixties, the Dow is pushing past twenty-six times gold. But behind the scenes, America is printing money to fund Vietnam and the Great Society. Other countries notice — France starts demanding gold for its dollars. The cracks are forming.

## Act 4: Nixon Shock & Gold's Liberation — 1971–1980 (3:34 – 4:40)

[VOICEOVER]
On August fifteenth, nineteen seventy-one, Nixon goes on television and severs the dollar from gold. The gold standard is dead.

Gold is free. And it explodes. From thirty-five dollars to over eight hundred in less than a decade.

Watch the race now. Every single line — every asset — plummets. The Dow-to-Gold ratio goes from twenty down to one. Everything else drops over ninety percent. Platinum — which started the race above gold — falls below it for the first time ever. Even the mighty DAX loses ninety-one percent of its gold value.

And then comes January twenty-second, nineteen eighty. The single most dramatic day in a hundred years of data. On that day, one ounce of gold could buy the entire Dow Jones Industrial Average. One to one. Every asset ratio bottomed out — simultaneously. Gold crushed everything at once.

## Act 5: The Great Reversal — 1980–2000 (4:40 – 5:57)

[VOICEOVER]
Then the pendulum swings the other way. Hard.

Volcker hikes interest rates to twenty percent. Inflation dies. Stocks take off. And gold just sits there — dead money — for twenty years.

The Dow-to-Gold ratio explodes from one to forty-five. It's the greatest stock bull run in history. The DAX goes from zero point three to twenty-seven — that's an eighty-seven-fold increase. And the Nikkei — here's where it gets truly absurd. Remember that tiny line near the bottom of the chart? Starting from almost nothing in 1949, the Nikkei goes eighty-four times higher — peaking at zero point six seven in December nineteen eighty-nine. The ratio is still a small number, but the growth is staggering. Eighty-four times.

Then Japan crashes. The Nikkei falls eighty-eight percent from that peak. Japan's miracle turns into Japan's lost decades.

Meanwhile, on August twenty-fifth, nineteen ninety-nine — the Dow-to-Gold ratio peaks at forty-four point eight eight. Gold's single worst day in a century. Nobody wants it. Stocks are king.

## Act 6: Gold's Revenge — 2000–2025 (5:57 – 7:27)

[VOICEOVER]
Then the twenty-first century begins — and the world starts breaking.

The dot-com crash starts the slide. Nine-eleven accelerates it. But two-thousand-eight is where it gets violent. Lehman Brothers collapses. Markets freefall. And gold — the asset nobody wanted in nineteen ninety-nine — surges past a thousand dollars an ounce.

The Dow-to-Gold ratio falls from forty-five back to about fourteen. The DAX drops from twenty-seven to eight. Britain's FTSE — which peaked at nearly thirty-nine times gold in nineteen ninety-nine — falls all the way back to just under nine. Up only forty percent over its entire existence. Barely positive — after more than thirty years.

Commodities get destroyed. Platinum — which started at nearly seven times gold in nineteen sixty-eight — hits an all-time low of zero point three three. Just a few weeks ago. A ninety-five percent collapse over the full dataset. Oil touches near zero in gold terms when prices go negative during COVID. Silver hits its lowest point versus gold during COVID — worse than the Great Depression.

Not a single commodity beat gold over this hundred years. Not one.

Second place — the S&P five hundred, up two hundred and thirty-seven percent. Third — the DAX, up two hundred and twenty-four percent. The Dow doubled. The FTSE barely held on.

The Dow-to-Gold ratio is cyclical — it swings between one and forty-five. Right now, we're at fourteen. Where it goes next — history says both directions are possible.

Nominal returns are an illusion. When you measure the world in gold — the picture looks very different.
