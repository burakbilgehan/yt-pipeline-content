## Act 1: The Hook & Setup (0:00 – 0:35)

[VOICEOVER]
The Dow Jones has gained twenty-six thousand percent since 1925. Sounds like the greatest investment in history, right? But what if I told you — when you price the Dow in gold — it has only doubled. That's it. One hundred years. One hundred and thirty-two percent. Less than one percent a year.

Here's the idea: forget dollars. Dollars lose value every single year. Instead, let's price everything — stocks, oil, silver, platinum — in gold. How many ounces of gold does it take to buy one unit of each asset? This strips away all the inflation noise and shows you what actually gained real value — and what didn't.

I crunched a hundred years of daily data — over a hundred and seventy-five thousand data points — and the results will change how you think about wealth. Let's race them.

## Act 2: The Gold Standard Era — 1925–1971 (0:35 – 1:20)

[VOICEOVER]
For the first half of this story, gold's price was fixed by the government. Twenty dollars and sixty-seven cents an ounce. So every ratio here is a pure reflection of supply and demand — no inflation tricks.

In 1925, the Dow-to-Gold ratio starts at six. Stocks are climbing. But then — the Great Depression. The ratio crashes. In 1934, Roosevelt devalues the dollar — gold jumps to thirty-five dollars overnight — and the ratio drops to three.

After World War Two, Bretton Woods pegs the entire global economy to gold. Slowly, stocks recover. By the late sixties, the Dow is pushing past twenty times gold. The Nikkei is at fifty-eight times gold — Japan's post-war miracle is real, and it's leading the race by a mile.

But behind the scenes, America is printing money to fund Vietnam. The gold standard is cracking.

## Act 3: Nixon Shock & Gold's Liberation — 1971–1980 (1:20 – 2:10)

[VOICEOVER]
On August fifteenth, nineteen seventy-one, Nixon goes on television and severs the dollar from gold. The gold standard is dead.

Gold is free. And it explodes. From thirty-five dollars to over eight hundred in less than a decade.

Watch the race now. Every single line — every asset — plummets. The Dow-to-Gold ratio goes from twenty down to one. The S&P from two point three to zero point one two. The DAX drops ninety-five percent. Even platinum — which was worth nearly seven times gold — falls below gold for the first time ever.

And then comes January twenty-second, nineteen eighty. The single most dramatic day in a hundred years of data. On that day, one ounce of gold could buy the entire Dow Jones Industrial Average. One to one. Every asset ratio bottomed out — simultaneously. Gold crushed everything at once.

## Act 4: The Great Reversal — 1980–2000 (2:10 – 2:50)

[VOICEOVER]
Then the pendulum swings the other way. Hard.

Volcker hikes interest rates to twenty percent. Inflation dies. Stocks take off. And gold just sits there — doing nothing — for twenty years.

The Dow-to-Gold ratio explodes from one to forty-five. It's the greatest stock bull run in history. The DAX goes from zero point five to twenty-eight. The Nikkei — remember, it was already leading the race — hits ninety-seven times gold in December nineteen eighty-nine. Ninety-seven. That's the Japanese asset bubble at its peak, and it's the highest any ratio ever reached in this entire dataset.

Then Japan crashes. The Nikkei-to-Gold ratio falls from ninety-seven to twelve. A loss of eighty-seven percent — even in gold terms. Japan's miracle turns into Japan's lost decades.

Meanwhile, on August twenty-fifth, nineteen ninety-nine — the Dow-to-Gold ratio peaks at forty-four point eight eight. Gold's single worst day in a century. Nobody wants it. Stocks are king.

Not a single commodity beat gold over this hundred years. Not one.
