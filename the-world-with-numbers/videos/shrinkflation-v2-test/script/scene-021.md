<break time="1200ms"/> Switch to the Consumer Price Index — CPI. This is the government's standard measure of inflation. It tracks roughly eighty thousand prices across the economy every month. When we use CPI instead of wages, we're asking a simpler question: did food prices outpace general inflation? <break time="500ms"/> The lines shift up. Chips crosses above one. Coffee jumps higher. Eggs climb further. Now three products cost more in inflation-adjusted terms than they did twenty-five years ago. <break time="500ms"/> Why the difference? Because median wages grew faster than inflation over this period — about a hundred and eleven percent versus eighty-seven percent. The median worker gained real purchasing power. That's a good thing. But it means the wage-deflated view is the OPTIMISTIC one.
