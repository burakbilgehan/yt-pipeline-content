And there's one more thing the data can't capture. Skimpflation. Same package, same size, same price — but cheaper ingredients. Thinner chips. Waterier ice cream. Less cocoa in the chocolate. Unlike shrinkflation, there's no government data series for this. No BLS measurement. It's real — economists acknowledge it — but it lives in the gap between what statistics can track and what your taste buds notice. <break time="500ms"/> If you were shopping during this decade, it probably didn't FEEL like things were getting cheaper. And in one sense, they weren't — per trip to the store. You were buying the same number of packages, paying roughly the same at checkout, getting less in the bag each time, and sometimes getting a lesser product too. The per-unit math works out. The grocery receipt doesn't care about per-unit math. <break time="1000ms"/> The lines are drifting down. Everything looks calm.
