<break time="500ms"/> And behind the scenes — something quieter is happening. Häagen-Dazs shrinks its container from sixteen to fourteen ounces. Skippy redesigns its peanut butter jar — same height, deeper indent in the bottom — dropping from eighteen to sixteen point three ounces. <break time="500ms"/> Why? Because three ninety-nine is a magic number. Consumers notice when a price crosses four dollars. They don't notice when two ounces disappear. The BLS tracks price per unit, so the shrinkage shows up in the data. But the sticker price at the register? That barely moved.
