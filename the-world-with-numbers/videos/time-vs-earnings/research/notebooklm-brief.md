# NotebookLM Research Brief — Time vs Earnings

## Context

I'm making a data-driven YouTube video (10-15 min, English) for a channel called "The World With Numbers." The channel presents data without editorial opinion — numbers speak for themselves.

## Video Concept

A 4-quadrant matrix plotting countries:
- **X axis:** Working hours (annual hours worked per worker)
- **Y axis:** Purchasing power (measured via Big Mac Index — how many Big Macs can you buy per hour of work)
- **Origin:** Global/OECD average
- **Quadrants:**
  - Top-right: Work a lot, earn a lot (e.g. USA?)
  - Top-left: Work little, earn a lot (e.g. Denmark, Germany?)
  - Bottom-right: Work a lot, earn little (e.g. Mexico, Greece?)
  - Bottom-left: Work little, earn little (?)

The video has two phases:
1. **Phase 1:** Explain purchasing power — why raw salary numbers are misleading. Use Big Mac Index as the normalization tool. A $3,000 salary means very different things in Turkey vs Switzerland.
2. **Phase 2 (the punchline):** Add the time dimension. "Now that you understand what people ACTUALLY earn... let's see how long they work for it." The matrix appears.

## What I Need You To Research

### 1. Big Mac Index — Deep Dive
- Latest Big Mac prices by country (The Economist publishes this, latest is Jan 2025)
- History and methodology of the Big Mac Index — who invented it, why, limitations
- Alternative purchasing power indexes (Starbucks Latte Index, KFC Index for Africa, iPad Index, etc.)
- Criticisms: Why is Big Mac Index imperfect? (McDonald's not in every country, local pricing strategies, Argentina manipulation scandal)
- The concept of PPP (Purchasing Power Parity) explained simply

### 2. Working Hours by Country
- Which countries work the most and least hours per year?
- Historical trends: How have working hours changed since 1950? Which countries reduced hours dramatically vs stayed flat?
- Why does the US work so much more than Europe despite similar GDP?
- Cultural factors: "996" in China, "karoshi" in Japan, siesta culture, Nordic work-life balance
- The 4-day workweek movement: Iceland trials, UK trials, Microsoft Japan experiment — results

### 3. Wages & Earnings by Country
- OECD average wages (PPP-adjusted) — latest data
- Countries where wages have stagnated (Italy, Japan, Greece)
- Countries with dramatic wage growth (Lithuania, Latvia, Poland)
- The gap between average and median wages (inequality signal)

### 4. Killer Combinations (for the matrix)
- Which countries are the biggest surprises when you combine hours + purchasing power?
- Are there countries that work very little AND earn very little? (bottom-left quadrant — who goes there?)
- Greece paradox: Most hours in EU, lowest hourly earnings
- Germany paradox: Few hours, very high purchasing power
- Any counter-intuitive findings

### 5. Human Stories & Anecdotes
- Real examples of what daily work life looks like in extreme cases (a worker in Sudan doing 2,600 hrs/yr vs Denmark at 1,380)
- WHO/ILO study: 745,000 deaths from overwork (2016)
- Any viral stories, studies, or cultural references about work-life balance that could hook viewers

### 6. Existing Videos & Content on This Topic
- Has anyone made a similar video? What angle did they take?
- What YouTube videos about "working hours by country" or "Big Mac Index" have done well? What was their hook?
- Competitive landscape: How can we differentiate?

## Output Format

Organize your findings as:
1. **Key data points** — numbers, stats, with sources
2. **Surprising findings** — things that would make a viewer say "wait, what?"
3. **Story hooks** — narrative threads that could structure the video
4. **Source list** — URLs, paper names, dataset names for fact-checking

## Important Notes
- Prioritize DATA over opinions. We want numbers, not think-pieces.
- Always note the year and source of any statistic.
- If something seems too good to be true, flag it.
- We're particularly interested in COUNTER-INTUITIVE findings — things that challenge the "work more = earn more" assumption.
