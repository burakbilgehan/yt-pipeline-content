## Section: What's Actually Safe (7:30–9:30)
[VOICEOVER]
So — after all that — what should you actually wear? <break time="1200ms"/>

Based on the research, the safest fabrics are organic cotton, linen, hemp, Tencel — which is a type of lyocell — and superfine merino wool. [pause]

Merino is worth highlighting. [pause short] At seventeen point five microns or finer, it doesn't just avoid irritation. A twelve-week clinical trial showed it actively REDUCES eczema symptoms. [pause long]

Wool can absorb up to thirty percent of its weight in moisture vapor without feeling damp. [pause short] Polyester does the opposite — it traps everything against your skin. [pause]

Linen and hemp are naturally antimicrobial. They don't need chemical treatments to resist bacteria. [pause short] Hemp is also the most durable natural fiber — and it softens with every wash. <break time="1200ms"/>

The FINISH matters as much as the fiber. [pause short] A chemically treated cotton shirt can be worse than untreated polyester. <break time="1200ms"/>

So what should you actually look for? [pause short] Third-party certifications. [pause short] Oeko-Tex Standard one hundred is one of the most recognized — it tests for over three hundred fifty harmful substances. [pause short] GOTS certification covers organic textiles from raw material to final product. [pause]

And when possible — choose unfinished, undyed fabrics. The fewer chemical treatments applied, the fewer chemicals end up on your skin.
