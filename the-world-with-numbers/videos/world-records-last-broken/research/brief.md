# Brief: The Last Time Every World Record Was Broken

Approved by the user on 27.09.2026 (idea stage). This file is the research stage's only input from that conversation.

## Question

Have humans stopped breaking world records? Concretely: for every athletics world record, when was it last broken, and do those dates show a limit?

## Angle: the frozen clock

Put the year each standing record was last set on one timeline. The story is the shape of that timeline: a cluster of records from the 1980s that are older than the athletes chasing them (expected to be mostly women's throws and a few sprints and jumps), a long quiet middle, and a second cluster since about 2019 in running events (road and track distance) plus a few outliers such as the pole vault. There is no single human limit: in some events the body seems to have stopped, in others the frontier is still moving and the reasons differ (equipment, tracks, pacing, professionalisation).

Closing beat: a 2008 study (believed to be Berthelot et al., "The Citius End", PLoS ONE) predicted that by 2027 half of all world records would be within 0.05 percent of their physiological limit. State what it predicted and how close we are in 2026. Do not settle the prediction in this video; a separate video in late 2027 will check it event by event.

## Audience takeaway

Where the records stopped, and when, tells you as much about history and equipment as about bodies. Some records have not moved in four decades; others fell last year.

## Scope of the data set

- All senior outdoor world records currently ratified by World Athletics, men and women. Primary source: the World Athletics records pages (worldathletics.org/records). Record each event, mark, holder, nationality, date and place.
- Indoor records are out of scope. Relays and race walks: collect them, but the timeline may show individual events only if that reads more clearly; note the choice.
- Pending (not yet ratified) marks: list them separately with their status, never mixed into the ratified set.
- For every record, note whether it was ever contested or annulled (doping sanctions, wind gauge disputes such as the women's 100 m of 1988) with the federation's or court's own record as the source. Facts only; no attribution of causes beyond what a federation, court or the athlete's own admission states.
