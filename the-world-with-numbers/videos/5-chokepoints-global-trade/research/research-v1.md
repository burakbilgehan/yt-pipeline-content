## Executive Summary

Five maritime chokepoints handle an estimated 55-60% of global seaborne oil trade. As of April 2026, the system is in unprecedented stress: the Strait of Hormuz is under full Iranian blockade (since March 2, 2026), Bab el-Mandeb faces renewed Houthi blockade threats, and the Suez Canal remains 60% below normal traffic. Oil prices surged ~63% in three weeks (Brent: $73 → $119 peak Mar 19, now ~$95).

**Key findings:**
- Malacca is the #1 oil chokepoint by volume (23.2 mb/d, 29% of global maritime oil) -- not Hormuz
- Hormuz is #2 by volume (20.9 mb/d) but #1 by crisis impact and current risk
- The 2026 Hormuz blockade is the largest oil supply disruption in history (up to 9.1 mb/d shut in)
- Pipeline bypass capacity for Hormuz is only ~9 mb/d vs 20.9 mb/d needed -- the gap is unbridgeable
- All five chokepoints have experienced disruptions in the past 3 years (2023-2026)
- Global cost of chokepoint disruptions: $14B/year (Oxford Martin), but the 2026 crisis dwarfs this baseline
- The cascade effect is real: Hormuz closure -> Saudi diverts to Yanbu -> increases Bab el-Mandeb dependency -> Houthis threaten that too
- China's "Malacca Dilemma" (80% of crude imports via one strait) drives billions in BRI infrastructure

**Most surprising stats for the video:**
- 6 days of one ship stuck in Suez = $79.6B estimated GDP impact
- One LPG carrier paid $2.85M for a single Panama Canal transit slot during the drought
- LNG carriers completely abandoned both Bab el-Mandeb (0.0 bcf/d) and Panama Canal (<0.3 bcf/d) -- and haven't come back even after conditions normalized

## Chokepoint 1: Strait of Hormuz
### Geography
- Length: ~104 miles (167 km)
- Width at narrowest: 21 miles (34-39 km)
- Depth: >100 meters in main shipping lanes (deep enough for largest crude tankers)
- Shipping lanes: Two 2-mile-wide lanes (inbound/outbound) separated by 2-mile median (Traffic Separation Scheme)
- Location: Between Iran (north) and Oman/UAE (south), connecting Persian Gulf to Gulf of Oman

### Transit Volume (EIA, 1H 2025)
- Oil: 20.9 million barrels/day (25% of all global maritime traded oil; ~20% of total global petroleum liquids consumption)
- LNG: >20% of world LNG trade (11.4 bcf/day)
- Vessel traffic: 138-140 ships/day (pre-blockade, early 2026)

### Key Dependents
- 89% of crude oil and condensate through the strait goes to Asia (EIA, 1H 2025)
- China, India, Japan, South Korea = 74% of all oil/condensate flows through Hormuz
- China: 33% of total oil imports via Hormuz
- Japan: ~95% of crude from Middle East; ~70% of that via Hormuz
- South Korea, India: major dependents but exact % not sourced [VERIFY]

### Historical Crises
- **1980-1988 Tanker War (Iran-Iraq War):** 8 years of naval/air attacks on commercial vessels. US Navy escorted convoys ("Operation Earnest Will"). High violence but strait never fully closed.
- **2008:** US-Iran tensions; Iran threat to close strait caused oil prices to jump >2%
- **2011-2012:** Iran threatened closure over EU oil sanctions; oil climbed briefly from 4-week low
- **2019 Tanker Seizures:** June 2019 -- two tankers struck mines; US drone shot down; July -- Iran seized British-flagged *Stena Impero*. [VERIFY: oil price impact -- sources didn't specify]
- **June 2025:** Following Israeli strikes, Iran threatened blockade (June 14, 2025) -- oil spiked 7%. Threat faded by June 23; oil fell back below $70/bbl.
- **February-April 2026 (ONGOING):** Full blockade -- see Current Status below.

### Price Impact
- 2026 blockade: Brent crude from $73.12/bbl (Feb 2026 avg) to >$100 (Mar 8) to peak ~$119/bbl (Mar 19) = ~63% increase
- This is the largest oil price spike from a chokepoint crisis in modern history
- Previous threats (2008, 2012, 2025) caused 2-7% spikes that quickly reversed
- [VERIFY: 1980s Tanker War oil price impact -- sources didn't specify exact figures for that period]

### Current Status (2026)
- **Feb 28, 2026:** US and Israel launched coordinated airstrikes on Iran ("Operation Epic Fury"), assassinating Supreme Leader Khamenei
- **March 2, 2026:** IRGC officially confirmed Strait of Hormuz closed
- Vessel traffic: dropped from 138-140 ships/day to near-zero
- **April 17:** Iran briefly declared strait open during temporary ceasefire
- **April 18:** Iran re-closed strait after US refused to lift naval blockade of Iranian ports
- **As of April 20, 2026: Blockade still ongoing**
- Impact: up to 9.1 million b/d of crude production shut in by April
- Shipping insurance: war risk premiums surged from 0.05% to 1.0% of cargo value (~$400M annually for industry)
- Freight rates more than doubled
- European natural gas: doubled from EUR 30/MWh to >EUR 60/MWh within days
- Also disrupted: aluminum, fertilizer, helium supply chains
- Knock-on: Egypt's Suez Canal revenues fell 60% ($7B loss) due to reduced overall vessel traffic

### Alternative Routes (if Hormuz closed)
- **Combined pipeline bypass capacity: ~9 million b/d** -- cannot replace the 20+ mb/d normal flow
  - UAE Habshan-Fujairah pipeline: ~2 million b/d (bypasses strait to Gulf of Oman)
  - Saudi East-West Pipeline (Petroline): 7 mb/d capacity, but only 4-5 mb/d available after domestic use (delivers to Red Sea port of Yanbu)
  - SUMED Pipeline (Egypt): 2.5 mb/d (Red Sea to Mediterranean, bypasses Suez)
  - Iran Goreh-Jask pipeline: ~0.3 mb/d (to Gulf of Oman terminal)
- **Critical vulnerability:** Diverting to Red Sea ports like Yanbu concentrates traffic in Bab el-Mandeb -- which is also under threat from Houthis
- Cape of Good Hope detour: adds 10-15 days transit time

## Chokepoint 2: Strait of Malacca
### Geography
- Length: ~550 miles (890 km) [VERIFY -- not in NotebookLM sources, commonly cited figure]
- Width at narrowest point (Phillips Channel near Singapore): ~1.7 miles (2.8 km) [VERIFY]
- Minimum depth: ~25 meters (82 ft) at shallowest -- limits fully laden supertankers (VLCCs must partially offload) [VERIFY]
- Location: Between Malay Peninsula (Malaysia) and Indonesian island of Sumatra; connects Indian Ocean to South China Sea
- NOTE: Exact geography dimensions were NOT available in NotebookLM sources. Above figures are commonly cited but flagged for verification.

### Transit Volume (EIA, 1H 2025)
- Oil: 23.2 million barrels/day = 29% of total global maritime oil flows (LARGEST oil chokepoint by volume, surpassing Hormuz)
- LNG: 9.2 bcf/day (Qatar-to-China LNG doubled since 2020; 28% of strait's LNG in 1H25)
- Vessel traffic: >60,000 vessels/year (~164+ vessels/day)
- Rebounded in 1H25 after declines from 2024 OPEC+ production cuts

### Key Dependents
- ~80% of oil imported to Northeast Asia passes through Malacca
- China: ~80% of imported crude oil via Malacca; in 1H25, China = 48% of all import volumes through the strait
- Japan, South Korea: major dependents within the NE Asia 80% figure (individual % not sourced)

### Historical Crises
- **Piracy:** Persistent threat. Incidents increased after 2023, particularly around Singapore (IMB Piracy Reporting Centre)
- **"Malacca Dilemma" (coined 2003):** Chinese leader Hu Jintao named China's strategic vulnerability to blockade of Malacca. Chinese strategic analysis views US naval presence as potential threat -- in a Taiwan conflict scenario, US could cut off China's petroleum imports via surface ships and submarines.
- No full closure in modern history -- but piracy and geopolitical tension are persistent

### Price Impact
- No major closure = no direct price spike data for Malacca
- However, the "Malacca Dilemma" drives billions in Chinese infrastructure investment to create bypass alternatives

### Current Status (2026)
- Operational as of April 2026
- Piracy still a concern (rising since 2023)
- Strategic tension: ongoing US-China competition; Taiwan risk elevates Malacca's geopolitical significance
- Flows rebounded after 2024 OPEC+ cuts lifted

### Alternative Routes
- **Sunda Strait:** Shallower, strong tidal currents, difficult navigation
- **Lombok Strait / Makassar Strait:** Viable but longer routes, increased shipping costs (exact distance penalty not sourced)

  - China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC): Gwadar Port to Xinjiang
- **Northern Sea Route (Arctic):** Not yet year-round viable; lacks infrastructure

## Chokepoint 3: Suez Canal
### Geography
- Length: ~120 miles (193 km) [VERIFY -- not in NotebookLM sources, commonly cited]
- No locks -- sea-level canal
- Max vessel draft: 20.1 meters (66 ft) = "Suezmax" class (~200,000 DWT)
- Location: Egypt, connecting Mediterranean Sea to Red Sea
- [VERIFY: exact width, depth of canal]

### Transit Volume
- Oil (EIA, 1H 2025): Suez Canal + SUMED pipeline combined = 4.9 million b/d
- Total vessel traffic: >19,000 vessels/year pre-crisis (2022 baseline)
- ~12% of global maritime trade flows through the Suez/Red Sea corridor

### Key Dependents
- Europe-Asia trade route: shortest maritime connection
- Egypt: Suez Canal tolls are a critical revenue source (lost $7B in 2024 alone from reduced traffic)
- [VERIFY: specific country dependency percentages]

### Historical Crises
- **1956 Suez Crisis:** Egypt nationalized the canal; France, Israel, UK military intervention. Canal closed for several months. Oil prices rose during closure but recovered after reopening.
- **1967-1975 Closure:** Canal closed for 8 years after Six-Day War. Forced permanent rerouting of global shipping around Cape of Good Hope. [VERIFY: specific trade/price impact during this period]
- **2021 Ever Given Blockage:**
  - Dates: March 23-29, 2021 (6-6.5 days)
  - $3-9 billion worth of freight delayed per day ($125-400M per hour)
  - Ship waiting/diversion costs: EUR 50-100M per day
  - ~400 ships disrupted globally; 200+ backed up at canal entrances
  - Direct economic damage: EUR 2-2.5 billion
  - Broader GDP impact estimate (IDE-GSM model): $79.6 billion net negative (~0.1% of world GDP), with $291.5B total negative offset by $211.9B positive effects for alternative-route regions
- **2023-2024 Houthi Red Sea Attacks:**
  - Started November 2023 (linked to Israel-Hamas conflict)
  - Canal passages fell 50% to 13,213 vessels in 2024
  - Egypt lost $7 billion in canal revenues (60% drop)
  - See Bab el-Mandeb section for full Houthi crisis details

### Price Impact
- **Ever Given (2021):** Surprisingly modest oil price impact -- mainly affected container/general cargo
- **Houthi crisis (2023-2024):** Freight rates >doubled, surpassing $4,000 per 40-ft container. War risk insurance doubled to 0.7% (peaked at 1% of vessel value)
- **1967-1975 closure:** Contributed to conditions leading to 1973 oil crisis but was not sole cause
- [VERIFY: exact oil price movements during 1956 and 1967 crises]

### Current Status (2026)
- As of early January 2026: Suez Canal traffic still 60% below pre-crisis (2023) levels
- Throughout 2025: transits by DWT remained 57-64% lower than 2023
- Product tankers returned faster: only 19% reduction in Q4 2025
- The 2026 Hormuz crisis has further complicated recovery

### Alternative Routes
- **Cape of Good Hope:** Primary alternative
  - Arab Gulf to Europe: 4,616 miles via Suez vs 11,354 miles via Cape = +6,738 miles extra
  - Adds 10-18.5 days transit time
  - 40% increase in fuel consumption
  - ~$1 million extra per voyage (fuel, crew, insurance)
- SUMED pipeline (bypasses canal for crude oil): 2.5 mb/d capacity

## Chokepoint 4: Panama Canal
### Geography
- Length: 50 miles (80 km)
- Narrowest point: Culebra Cut, ~9 miles long at the Continental Divide
- Lock-based canal (not sea-level) -- depends on freshwater from Gatun Lake
- Max vessel size: "Panamax" (original locks) and "Neopanamax" (new locks, opened 2016) [VERIFY: exact beam/draft limits -- Panamax typically 32.3m beam, 12m draft; Neopanamax 49m beam, 15.2m draft]
- Location: Panama, connecting Atlantic (Caribbean) to Pacific Ocean

### Transit Volume (EIA, FY 2025)
- Oil: 2.3 million b/d (3% of global maritime petroleum flows)
  - Mainly refined products (2.2 mb/d), not crude -- products travel on smaller vessels better suited for narrow canal
- LNG: collapsed from 2.5 bcf/d (FY 2021) to <0.3 bcf/d (FY 2025) -- vessels permanently rerouted during drought and haven't returned
- [VERIFY: total vessel transits per year -- sources didn't specify]

### Key Dependents
- US Gulf Coast to East Asia: primary route for hydrocarbon gas liquids (propane, ethane) to Asian petrochemical markets
- US Gulf Coast LNG to western South America and Asia
- Post-2026 Iran war: European buyers (e.g., Germany's Uniper) exploring Canadian Pacific LNG via Panama Canal to diversify from Middle East

### Historical Crises
- **2023-2024 Drought:**
  - October 2023: driest on record in Panama since 1950
  - Gatun Lake levels dropped, forcing Panama Canal Authority to cut daily transit slots to ~50% of normal capacity by February 2024
  - Large oil tankers and "tramp ships" effectively abandoned the route
  - Bidding wars for remaining slots: one LPG carrier paid $2.85 million for a single transit slot
  - Recovery: oil product flows recovered in FY 2025 after water levels returned to historical averages
  - [VERIFY: total economic cost of the drought -- sources didn't provide]

### Price Impact
- Drought drove auction premiums to extreme levels ($2.85M for one slot)
- LNG flows collapsed and have NOT recovered even after water levels normalized -- permanent behavioral shift
- Less of an oil price mover than Hormuz/Suez because only 3% of maritime petroleum flows
- [VERIFY: any freight rate or commodity price spike data from the 2023-2024 drought]

### Current Status (2026)
- Oil product flows recovered in FY 2025
- LNG transit still severely depressed (<0.3 bcf/d vs 2.5 bcf/d pre-drought) -- vessels permanently rerouted
- **New threat (April 2026):** El Nino Watch issued -- signals weather conditions that could trigger another severe drought and capacity squeeze
- Trans-Panama Pipeline operating as partial bypass for crude

### Alternative Routes
- **Southern South America:** Straits of Magellan, Cape Horn, Drake Passage -- adds up to 8,000 miles; dangerous waterways
- **Eastward:** Cape of Good Hope or Suez Canal -- adds thousands of miles
- **Trans-Panama Pipeline:** Land-based crude oil alternative; capacity 864,000 b/d; transported ~400,000 b/d during 2024-2025

## Chokepoint 5: Bab el-Mandeb Strait
### Geography
- Width at narrowest: 18 miles (30 km)
- Length: ~20 miles (32 km) [VERIFY -- not in NotebookLM sources]
- Depth: [VERIFY -- not sourced]
- Location: Between Yemen (Arabian Peninsula) and Djibouti/Eritrea (Africa); connects Red Sea to Gulf of Aden/Indian Ocean
- Effectively the "gateway" to the Suez Canal -- anything going through Suez must first pass Bab el-Mandeb

### Transit Volume
- **Pre-crisis (2023):** 9.3 million b/d oil; 4.2 bcf/d LNG
- **Post-Houthi attacks (1H 2025):** 4.2 million b/d oil (55% drop); LNG = 0.0 bcf/d (complete abandonment by LNG carriers)
- Container traffic dropped ~75% in 2024
- Vessels per year: linked to Suez figures (>19,000 pre-crisis)

### Key Dependents
- Saudi Arabia: relies on strait to export oil from Red Sea port of Yanbu to Asia
- Asia-Europe trade corridor: critical link
- Disproportionate impact on emerging economies:
  - Pakistan: 8% drop in textile exports due to shipping delays
  - Sudan: 30% increase in humanitarian aid costs
  - Kenya, Tanzania: GDP drops from delayed western trade
- Egypt: $7B loss from Suez revenue collapse

### Historical Crises
- **2023-2024 Houthi Attacks (MAJOR):**
  - November 19, 2023: Houthis hijacked *MV Galaxy Leader* (Bahamas-flagged), 25 crew held hostage
  - January 9, 2024: Massive drone and missile barrage (130+ attacks by this point)
  - February 18, 2024: Bulk carrier *MV Rubymar* struck; sank March 2, 2024 ($80M insurance claim)
  - 70% decline in Red Sea shipping traffic (Dec 2023 - Apr 2024)
  - Container vessel traffic specifically: -75%
  - Last successful attack: *Minervagracht*, late September 2025
  - Houthis declared end to attacks 43 days later
- [VERIFY: any pre-2023 Houthi or other Bab el-Mandeb disruptions?]

### Price Impact
- Global shipping costs: surged 30% in 2023
- Shanghai-Rotterdam index: spiked 250% in December 2023
- War risk insurance: doubled from 0.3% to 0.7%, peaked at 1% of vessel value
- Freight rates: >$4,000 per 40-ft container (doubled)
- All major carriers diverted (CMA CGM, Maersk, others)

### Current Status (2026)
- Houthi attacks paused since late 2025, but recovery slow
- As of January 2026: Red Sea/Suez traffic still 60% below normal
- **New escalation (April 2026):** Following US-Israel-Iran war and Hormuz blockade, Houthis threatened FULL blockade of Bab el-Mandeb to choke off Saudi alternative oil exports (Yanbu) -- threatening to trap 4+ million b/d of oil
- This creates a dual chokepoint crisis: Hormuz AND Bab el-Mandeb simultaneously threatened

### Alternative Routes
- **Cape of Good Hope:** Primary alternative
  - Adds 10-15 days to Asia-Europe voyages
  - Slashes route capacity by 15-20%
  - 40% increase in fuel consumption
  - ~$1 million extra per voyage
